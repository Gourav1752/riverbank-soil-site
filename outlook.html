Sections 18–21

Significance, limitations and what comes next

Post-flood soil assessment supports both farming and environmental protection. Extended into continuous monitoring, it could predict where deficiencies will appear after the next flood.

Section 18 · Environmental significance

Why nutrient balance matters beyond the field

Excessive nutrient loss reduces soil fertility. Excessive accumulation of nutrients or contaminants can harm crops and nearby water bodies. Soil nutrient balance affects:

  • Agricultural productivity
  • Vegetation growth
  • Riverbank ecosystems
  • Soil quality
  • Groundwater quality
  • Nutrient cycling
  • Long-term land sustainability

Section 19 · Limitations

What can influence the results

Consistent sampling and laboratory procedures are important for reliable results.

  • Soil typeDifferences between sampling points
  • Flood durationLength of waterlogging
  • Floodwater qualityWhat the water carried
  • Sediment compositionWhat was deposited
  • Sampling locationSame spot or only nearby
  • Sampling depthConsistency between samples
  • Drainage conditionsHow fast water left the field
  • Time to samplingGap between recession and post-flood sampling

Section 20 · Conclusion

Conclusion

Flooding can significantly modify the mineral and nutrient composition of soil near riverbanks, through leaching, erosion, runoff, sediment deposition, waterlogging and changes in soil chemical conditions.

Comparing soil samples collected before and after flooding is a practical way to find which nutrients have decreased, increased or remained relatively stable. That information shows how soil fertility has changed and what is needed to restore it.

Post-flood crop selection should follow the condition of the soil and the prevailing moisture and drainage. Rice may suit fields where wet conditions persist, while wheat, mustard, maize and pulses can be considered after adequate drainage. Urea, SSP/DAP and MOP can be considered where nitrogen, phosphorus and potassium deficiencies are identified, and compost and farmyard manure help restore soil structure and organic matter.

Overall, the project provides a systematic framework linking flooding, soil nutrient dynamics, crop selection, fertilizer management, agriculture and environmental sustainability.

Technology

A system that predicts deficiencies

A future system could collect environmental and soil data continuously and predict which areas are likely to become nutrient-deficient after flooding.

Collect

  • IoT-based soil sensors
  • GPS-based sampling
  • Weather and rainfall data
  • River-water monitoring
  • Remote sensing

Map

  • GIS soil-nutrient mapping

Predict and recommend

  • Machine-learning models
  • Automated nutrient-deficiency prediction
  • Crop recommendation systems
