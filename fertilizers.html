Sections 15, 16, 17 and 19

Fertilizers, amendments and soil management

Base every fertilizer decision on the post-flood soil test. Excess fertilizer applied without knowing the soil’s nutrient status is lost from the soil, wastes money and pollutes the environment.

Section 15 · Recommendations

Sources by nutrient

Nitrogen 15.1 · if deficiency is confirmed

Supports leaf development, plant growth and biomass production.

Apply nitrogen after excess water has drained, rather than while the soil remains heavily waterlogged.

Sources
  • Urea
  • Ammonium sulfate
  • Other locally recommended N fertilizers

Phosphorus 15.2 · where deficiency is identified

Particularly important for root development and early crop establishment.

DAP also supplies nitrogen, so include its nitrogen when calculating the overall fertilizer requirement.

Sources
  • Single Super Phosphate (SSP)
  • Diammonium Phosphate (DAP)

Potassium 15.3 · where deficiency is confirmed

Supports water regulation, stress tolerance and the plant’s structural strength.

Source
  • Muriate of Potash (MOP / KCl)

Sulfur 15.4

Can be particularly important for crops such as mustard.

Sources
  • Gypsum
  • Ammonium sulfate
  • SSP

Organic amendments 15.5

Help restore soil structure and organic matter after flooding. Organic matter supports soil structure, moisture management and microbial activity.

Options
  • Farmyard manure (FYM)
  • Well-decomposed compost
  • Vermicompost
  • Green manure

Section 17 · Post-flood soil management strategy

Test first, then apply

Follow a systematic process rather than applying fertilizer immediately after the flood. It prevents unnecessary fertilizer use and supports efficient soil restoration.

  1. Soil sampling
  2. Laboratory soil testing
  3. Identification of nutrient deficiencies
  4. Assessment of soil moisture, pH and EC
  5. Selection of a suitable crop
  6. Selection of the required fertilizer or organic amendment
  7. Application according to the soil-test recommendation
  8. Crop growth monitoring
  9. Follow-up soil testing
