Sections 5, 8 and 9

Methodology

Soil is sampled at fixed points at increasing distance from the river, once before the flood and again after the water recedes, then analysed in the laboratory for the same set of parameters.

Section 5 · Study area and scope

Choosing the site

The study should be conducted near a riverbank where flooding occurs periodically.

The selected study area should preferably include:

  • Riverbank soilSoil directly exposed to floodwater
  • Agricultural or vegetated landLand where fertility matters for crops
  • Historically flooded areasPlaces known to be affected by past floods
  • Several distances from the riverMultiple sampling locations along a line from the bank
  • Accessible sampling pointsReachable both before and after the flood

Sampling zones

Three representative zones

Soil should be collected from approximately the same depth at each location so the comparison is meaningful.

SampleLocationPurpose
Sample AClose to the riverbankAssess direct flood impact
Sample BMedium distance from the riverAssess intermediate impact
Sample CFarther from the riverProvide comparison / reference

Section 8 · Research methodology

Four stages

  1. Select the study area 8.1

    Choose a riverbank region that floods periodically. Identify three or more representative sampling locations according to their distance from the river.

  2. Sample before the flood 8.2

    Collect soil from the predetermined locations: A near the riverbank, B at medium distance, C farther away. Keep sampling depth and collection procedure the same at every location.

  1. Sample after the flood 8.3

    Once the floodwater has receded and the land is accessible, collect soil from the same locations, or as close to them as practically possible.

  2. Analyse in the laboratory 8.4

    Analyse every sample for the parameters below, using standard soil-testing kits or laboratory procedures depending on the facilities available.
