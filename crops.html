Section 14 · Selection criteria

What decides the crop

  • Soil moistureHow wet the field still is
  • Drainage conditionsWhether excess water has gone
  • Soil nutrient statusDeficiencies from the soil test
  • Soil pHChanges since the flood
  • Flood durationHow long the land was under water
  • SeasonKharif or rabi timing
  • Degree of soil damageErosion, compaction, deposition

Section 14.1 · Recommended crops

Crop suitability after flooding

Rice, maize, wheat, mustard, pulses and vegetables are potential post-flood crops depending on moisture and drainage.

CropSuitability after floodingMajor nutrient requirements
Rice / paddySuitable for areas where wet conditions persistN, P, K
MaizeSuitable after excess water has drainedParticularly N, along with P and K
WheatSuitable during the appropriate post-monsoon / rabi season after drainageN, P, K
MustardSuitable in adequately drained soilN, P, S
ChickpeaSuitable where soil has adequate drainageP and other nutrients; generally low starter N
LentilSuitable after sufficient drainageP and other nutrients; generally low starter N
Green gramShort-duration option after flood recessionP, K and micronutrients
Black gramSuitable where adequate drainage existsP, K and micronutrients
VegetablesCan be cultivated after the soil becomes suitableCrop-specific

Section 14.2 · Importance of pulses

Why pulses help a post-flood rotation

Chickpea, lentil, green gram and black gram are legumes. They form associations with nitrogen-fixing bacteria and can help improve the soil’s nitrogen status after a flood has depleted it.

  • Chickpea
  • Lentil
  • Green gram
  • Black gram

Pulses still need other nutrients

Biological nitrogen fixation does not remove the need for all fertilizer inputs. Phosphorus and other nutrient requirements still have to be met.
