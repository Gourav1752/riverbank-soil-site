Project report · Soil science & agriculture

What a flood takes from riverbank soil, and what it leaves behind

Floodwater can strip nutrients through leaching, runoff, erosion and denitrification while depositing sediment and organic matter that adds others. This project compares soil tested before and after a flood to find out which nutrients became deficient, which accumulated, and how to restore fertility.

Section 1 · Abstract

A flood does not affect every nutrient the same way

Flooding influences the physical, chemical and biological characteristics of soil, especially on agricultural land close to riverbanks. Floodwater can cause nutrient loss through leaching, runoff, erosion and denitrification, and at the same time deposit sediments and organic matter that raise the concentration of certain nutrients.

This project assesses and compares the mineral and nutrient status of soil before and after flooding. Soil samples are collected at different distances from the riverbank, and laboratory analysis shows which nutrients have become deficient and which have accumulated.

Based on the post-flood soil condition, suitable crops can be selected and the right fertilizers or organic amendments recommended. The study supports better soil management, restoration of soil fertility, agricultural productivity and environmental sustainability in flood-prone riverbank regions.

Section 3 · Problem statement

Questions the study answers

Nutrients may be lost through leaching, runoff, erosion and denitrification, while floodwater may also deposit minerals and organic matter. Prolonged waterlogging can change soil pH, oxygen availability and the chemical forms of nutrients.

  • How does flooding affect overall soil fertility?
  • Which crops are suitable for cultivation after flooding?
  • Which fertilizers or organic amendments are required to restore soil fertility?

Section 4 · Aim and objectives

Aim

To analyze and compare soil mineral and nutrient deficiencies before and after flooding in areas located near a riverbank, and evaluate the effect of flooding on soil fertility.

Objectives

  1. Identify a suitable riverbank area for soil assessment.
  2. Collect soil samples from selected locations before flooding.
  3. Collect soil samples from the same or comparable locations after flooding.
  4. Determine the concentration of major and micronutrients in soil.
  5. Compare nutrient concentrations before and after flooding.
  6. Identify nutrients that become deficient or increase following flooding.
  7. Study changes in soil pH, electrical conductivity and organic matter.
  8. Assess the potential effect of nutrient changes on agricultural productivity.
  9. Identify suitable crops for cultivation after flooding.
  10. Recommend suitable fertilizers and soil amendments based on soil-test results.
  11. Propose methods for long-term soil fertility restoration and monitoring.
