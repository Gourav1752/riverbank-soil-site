Section 11 · Data analysis

How the sheet calculates change

Absolute change

Change = After-flood value − Before-flood value

Percentage change

% change = [(After − Before) / Before] × 100

Worked example: potassium

Before flooding150 mg/kg
After flooding120 mg/kg
Percentage change[(120 − 150) / 150] × 100 = −20%

The measured potassium concentration decreased by 20%. The same calculation applies to every other parameter.

Sections 12–13 · Interpretation framework

Reading the before vs. after comparison

Each parameter either increased, decreased or stayed relatively stable. Flooding should not automatically be read as causing only nutrient loss; the result depends on the site.

ObservationPossible interpretation
Nutrient concentration decreasesPossible leaching, runoff, erosion or plant uptake
Nutrient concentration increasesPossible sediment deposition or mineral accumulation
Organic matter increasesPossible deposition of organic-rich sediments
pH changesPossible alteration of soil chemical conditions
EC increasesPossible salt / mineral accumulation
Nitrogen decreasesPossible leaching or denitrification
Potassium decreasesPossible mobility and removal through floodwater

Deficiency status comes from real test results

Leaching, erosion, runoff, denitrification and removal of topsoil can all cause loss, but flood sediment may add nutrients and organic matter at the same time. Decide the final deficiency status from actual post-flood soil-test results, not from an assumption that every nutrient falls.
